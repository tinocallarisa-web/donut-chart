A donut and pie chart that reads at any size: labels inside every slice with the same content and text size and never cut, percentages that add up to 100, an honest Other you can open, a legend and a data table you can place where you want, and the total in the center. Pro adds the change in share and value against another period, targets with ✓ ≈ ✕, an outer Segment ring, annotations, small multiples and six style templates.

Quick Start

  1. Add Donut & Pie Chart by TCViz to the page. With no fields it shows a start screen with these steps.
  2. Drag the field that names the slices to Category — brand, region, channel.
  3. Drag the measure the slices share to Values — sales, litres, units.
  4. While you edit, the setup guide in the corner lists the optional fields and lets you try the styles.
  5. Pro: add last year to Compare with and the plan to Target; add a second field to Segment or Small multiples.

Sample data used in the screenshots: DonutChart_Dataset.xlsx (Begudes Nord, a fictitious drinks company): sheet Ventas with brand, family, zone, channel, litres, last year's litres and the plan.

Three to eight categories read best as a donut. With more, or with a hierarchy, a treemap reads better.

How to read it

The angle of each slice is its share of the total. The labels and the table use the same percentages, rounded so they add up to 100. Other is always last and grey. What is not drawn — negative, zero or blank values, rows Power BI did not send — is said in a footnote.

Selection and interaction

  • Click a slice, a legend item or a table row to filter the rest of the page. Ctrl/Cmd + click adds; click the background to clear.
  • Lasso selection: drag on the chart to draw a rectangle and select every slice it touches, in every panel; Ctrl or Shift + drag adds. Turn it off in Format → Slices.
  • Clicking Other filters by every item inside it. In the data table, Other ▸ opens its members, and each one can be selected.
  • With Segment, the outer ring selects the category and segment pair.
  • The center shows what you point at or select; with several slices selected, their sum.
  • Highlight: when another visual filters this one, the highlighted part of each slice is drawn and the rest is dimmed, Other included.
  • Bookmarks restore the selection. Right-click opens the Power BI context menu. A hierarchy in Category gives Drilldown.
  • Click a header of the data table to sort by it; click again to reverse. The order is not saved with the report.
  • Report page tooltips are supported. When the report author turns interactions off, clicks do not filter.

Accessibility

  • Keyboard: Tab into the visual (one stop); arrows move from slice to slice, with the focus on the slice and on its table row; Home/End jump to the first/last; Enter or Space selects, Ctrl+Enter adds; Esc clears; → on Other opens it and ← closes it; Shift+F10 or the context-menu key opens the menu.
  • A live region reads the name, value, share and, with Pro, the change and the target of the focused slice; the summary sentence is part of the description.
  • High contrast: the visual uses the colours of the Windows contrast theme, with every slice outlined and patterns on.
  • Patterns tell slices apart without relying on colour; targets use ✓ ≈ ✕ and the mix warning uses ⚠, shapes as well as colours.
  • A minimum text size (8–16 px) applies to every text; focus colour and width can be set.
  • Reduce motion turns off the fades on hover and selection; they are also off when the system asks for reduced motion.
  • Text scales with the size of the visual, and the layout adapts: legend and table move, labels shorten, rows fold into "+N more" in very small visuals.

FAQ

Why does a slice have no label?

Every label has the same content and the same text size, so they read as one set. When the legend or the data table is visible, labels always go inside the slices: the text gets smaller down to the minimum, then the content gets shorter (the change, then the value, then the name; the percent goes last). A slice too thin for even the shortest label is left without one — never with a cut text — and its figure stays in the table and the tooltip. With the legend and the table off, a label that does not fit inside goes outside with a leader line.

Why do the percentages add up to exactly 100?

Rounded independently, percentages often add up to 99 or 101. The visual rounds by the largest remainder method, so the labels, the table and the total row always add up to 100 at the decimals you choose. The tooltip uses the same rounding.

What is Other, and how do I open it?

Categories beyond Show at most (6 by default) or below Group below % (3% by default) go into Other, always the last slice, with its count: "Other (5)". In the data table, click Other (5) ▸ to list its members with their own values and percentages (→ on the keyboard). The five largest categories always keep their own slice, even below Group below %, so a donut is never all Other. Clicking the Other slice filters the page by every item inside it, not by a label. Set Show at most to 20 and Group below to 0 to draw every category.

Why is a value not drawn?

A slice is an angle of a whole, and it cannot be negative or empty. Negative, zero and blank values are left out and counted in a footnote — "Excluded: 1 negative (−0.3M), 1 blank" — so nothing disappears in silence. Percentages use the positive total.

What does the center show?

The total of Values, with the measure name as its title (or your own title). Point at a slice, select it or move to it with the keyboard and the center shows its name, value and percent; with several slices selected, it shows how many and their sum. The pie has no center. With small multiples each donut shows its own total.

Legend or Data table?

They are independent and can show together. The Legend is the classic one: swatch and name. The Data table adds the value and the share of every slice, the Pro columns and the footnotes. In Automatic the table appears when Compare with, Target or Annotation has a field; set its position to Right, Left or Bottom to show it without them. Click a header to sort the table and the slices; the order is not saved with the report.

How do I show the change against last year?

Add the same measure for the prior period to Compare with (Pro) — for example with SAMEPERIODLASTYEAR, with one year in the filter context. The data table adds Δ pp (the change in share, in percentage points, with a mini bar) and Δ % (the change in value); its title gives the change of the total. Labels add the Δ pp when it fits. A category with no prior value says new; one with no current value is listed as gone, never with an invented change. A ⚠ marks the rows where share and value move in opposite directions — value up but share down because the total grew faster — with a footnote that names them.

Every category says "new", or the change does not show. Why?

The Compare with measure returns blank, usually because the time-intelligence function has no single year in context. Filter the page or the visual to one year, or use a prior-year column in the model.

How do targets work?

Add the plan or budget to Target (Pro). The table shows the % of target of each category with ✓ (at or above Met at, 100% by default), ≈ (at or above Close at, 90%) or ✕ (below) — a shape and a colour, so it does not rely on colour. Status on slices puts the icon on the outer edge of the slices that miss the target, of all of them, or of none. The summary says how many categories miss it.

What does Segment do?

Segment (Pro) adds an outer ring: each slice is split by a second field at the same angle — family by brand, channel by subchannel. The ring has its own labels (Format → Data labels → Segment labels) and the table can list segment rows under each category. When every category belongs to a single segment (for example Brand in Category and Family in Segment), the ring would say nothing: it is not drawn and, while you edit, a note suggests swapping the two fields.

How do small multiples work?

Add a field to Small multiples (Pro). Every donut uses the same Other, order and colours, so you compare like with like; each one shows its own total in the center and its title above. The data table is shared and shows the total of all panels (its title says Total); the tooltip of a table row gives the value in each panel. Size by total makes the area of each donut proportional to its total. When the panels would be too small to read, the visual shows the ones that fit and says how many: "Showing N of M panels".

What does the Summary sentence say?

The leader and its share, and what the top three add up to; with Pro, the biggest shift against Compare with and how many categories miss the target. It uses the same rounded figures as the labels and is read by screen readers. Automatic shows it when Compare with has a field; turn it On or Off in Format → Summary.

Can I use conditional formatting (fx)?

Yes, in the free version: Format → Slices → Colors → All slices (fx) colours every slice by a rule or a field; a colour set for one category takes precedence. The border colour and the table header background accept fx too.

Why does a tooltip measure not show?

Measures in Tooltips show when a slice is one row of data. With Segment or Small multiples a slice is made of several rows, and adding up a measure such as a margin % or an average across them would give a false figure, so it is left out. Use a report page tooltip instead: it is computed by Power BI for the slice.

Why does a selection say "shown here only"?

With Segment or Small multiples, a selection is passed to Power BI as rows. Above 5,000 rows — for example Other over tens of thousands of points of sale — filtering the rest of the page would slow Power BI down, so the selection is drawn in the visual only and a note says so. Select fewer slices, or filter the page with a slicer.

How many rows can it use?

Power BI sends up to 30,000 combinations of category, segment and panel at a time. When there are more, the visual asks for further blocks, up to 10 more, and adds them up (checked in Power BI Desktop with 54,662 rows). While it loads, a footnote says "Loading more rows"; if rows are still missing, it says "Showing 30,000 rows; Other is incomplete".

Can I change the font?

Yes: the labels, the center (title and value), the legend, the data table, the summary, the footnotes and the small multiples titles each have a font, size and style. Blank uses the font of the style.

What is the "Pro preview" watermark?

While you edit a report without a licence, Pro features you turn on are drawn working, on your own data, under a watermark that names them, and Power BI shows its licence notice; small multiples show their first 4 panels. Reading view shows the free chart. Where Power BI provides no licence information (for example when you are not signed in to Power BI Desktop), the free chart is shown.

How do I get Pro?

On the visual's Microsoft AppSource page, choose Buy now (not Install free): 0.99 USD per user per month or 9.99 USD per user per year, with no trial. Licences are per user and managed by Microsoft; restart Power BI Desktop after the licence is assigned. See tcviz.com/licensing.

How are numbers formatted?

With the number format of the measure from the model, dynamic format strings included, in the report's language. A measure with no format gets thousands separators and no stray decimals. The labels, the center and the table each have their own display units and decimals.

Is my data sent anywhere?

No. The visual makes no network requests, declares no privileges and stores nothing outside your report.
